Arms Laid Bair

Seasons, people, and prayers hardly leave a mark. Drops of Amrita and the tree has sprung as a gift. At the neem's base, I sit in meditation with or without a bath. A chatak bird drinks raindrops and by the merits of past births I hear its tunes. The weight of 15 Rupees sits heavily in my pocket. Buying cloth and walapadi, on this day, I leave aside rice. I notice dogs in the shade and cooking fires of passing houses. The sound of the bell from another place of worship carries on the air. The ground shifts from shade to direct sun and back as the path bends. In the Masjid, a Dhuni flame dances and sparkles. My pronated hand moves towards it. A touch is met with a harsh kiss. I draw my hand away and watch it spin, ripple, and offer warmth. I take ash and place it onto my forehead. Casting aside sloth and laziness, I bring a bucket to the well and ladle water, adding petals for sweetness before drinking.

From the same bucket, I place oil upon the flames and watch as the fire is coaxed with new life. With light taps of my Satka, I reduce the flame and begin peeling plantains and mangoes. Almonds, walnuts, pistachios all boiled into milk. A person with lucky dishes to eat, my joy knows no bounds. He who concentrates his mind can attain samadhi even briefly. To ask for difficulties and get the solution. A child asks for sweets and receives bitter pills. The former spoil its health and the latter improves it. To cast aside ego breeds nothing but dismay. When a mother kicks their child and the sea turns to rivers is the same in which a proud man bows. If the path is difficult then I pray my own wilted litanies. I tap my Satka and the flame tempers.

I prepare my seat and bed and separate petals from the flower head. Letting them simmer before straining into the suspension with my hand. Dried in shade rather than direct sun to prevent the color from shifting too quickly. Warm and amber variations show upon the surface. In its wear, I remove each limb and toss each into the ashes. Nothing harms those with their focus affixed onto me. So that the mind may not wander. On my sleeve sits intention and truth, nothing but the truth. I do not know what I am. But to those who sing my leelas, I could offer diamonds. To all, I offer nascent gems.
