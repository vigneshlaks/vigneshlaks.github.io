Arms Laid Bair

In the temple, a dhuni flame dances and sparkles. My hands move towards it and the heat speckles my palms. Unabashed, I take ash and place it onto my forehead and bring a bucket to the well before ladling water. Adding petals for sweetness before drinking. From the same bucket, I place oil upon the flames and watch as the fire is coaxed with new life.

With light taps of my satka, I reduce the flame and begin peeling plantains and mangoes. Almonds, walnuts, pistachios boiled into milk. The stone quern cleaves through the otherwise silent occasion. I prepare my seat and bed before separating petals from the flower head. Letting them simmer before straining the dye into a suspension with my hand. Dried in shade rather than sun to prevent the color from shifting too quickly. Warm and amber variations show upon the surface. During the day's procession, I offer my evening's labor. We laugh, share stories, play games.

That particular day still lived in him with a sharp clarity. Walking through a side street, he saw a beggar sitting against a wall. The man had eaten. His belly was full. He was joking with someone, laughing openly, his face unguarded and light. Something broke open in him at the sight. He turned to the friends walking with him and spoke of the madness of their shared frenzy. How they dragged themselves forward under the spell of ambition, increasing their own misery with every step, all in the hope of reaching a happiness the beggar had already found with a few begged coins.

The beggar was carefree; he was full of fear. Asked which state he preferred, he would have said merriment. Asked whether he would rather be the beggar or remain himself, he would have chosen his own anxious, learned self. Learning had hardly offered solace; it had only been a means of pleasing.

What, finally, were all these labors for? What height could ambition reach except the brittle favor of the empowered, bought through a chain of lesser perils? And when, if ever, would a man arrive? A friend of God, if he wished it, he could become in a single moment. Kindled by a gift not his own, he felt himself lifted, glowing from within.
